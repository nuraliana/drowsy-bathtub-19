# Candidate jokes

Eight one-line cat jokes, scored honestly against the five judge criteria, each out of 5:

1. **Setup + turn** — a clear setup with a turn that lands.
2. **Laugh-out-loud** — funny on first read, not just clever.
3. **Surprise / freshness** — misdirection or an angle that feels new.
4. **Concision** — few words, no padding, no explanation after the punchline.
5. **Retellability** — easy to repeat to a friend the same day.

Well-worn angles were left out of this list on purpose; every candidate below tries a different scene.

| # | Candidate | Setup + turn | Laugh | Surprise | Concision | Retellability | Total |
|---|-----------|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | My cat fell in the bathtub once. She has held it against me for four years, which is fair — I held the camera. | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | **25** |
| 2 | The vet said my cat should lose weight, so now she glares at me like I invented both the scale and the diet. | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 19 |
| 3 | I put my suitcase on the bed and my cat sat on it, which is her way of saying the trip is cancelled. | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 20 |
| 4 | My cat only sits on my paperwork when the printer is jammed, as if she is the supervisor now. | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 17 |
| 5 | My cat rides the robot vacuum like a chauffeur service she never tipped for and never will. | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 19 |
| 6 | My cat unmuted me on a work call mid-sneeze, so my whole team now knows exactly what I sound like. | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 18 |
| 7 | My cat waits by the door for every delivery like she ordered it, then acts betrayed when it is mine. | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 19 |
| 8 | My cat met herself in the mirror and hissed, which is the first honest review she has ever given anyone. | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 19 |

## Why #1 wins

The bathtub joke is the only one where a single word carries the whole punchline: "held" means the cat holding a grudge and the human holding the camera at the same time. The setup is an ordinary accident, the turn reframes four years of resentment as earned, and the last clause admits guilt without explaining the joke. It is short, it needs no setup beyond the sentence itself, and it is easy to retell word for word — which is why it scores 5 on every criterion.

The others are decent but each gives something away. The suitcase joke (#3) is the closest runner-up, but its turn is telegraphed by the cat sitting down. The vet joke (#2) leans on a familiar blame-the-owner beat, the printer joke (#4) needs the reader to picture an office, and the work-call joke (#6) spends too many words getting to the sneeze. None of them has a second meaning doing the work of the punchline.
